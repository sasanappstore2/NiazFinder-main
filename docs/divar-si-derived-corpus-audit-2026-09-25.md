# Divar → Si real-estate corpus audit (2026-09-25)

## Decision and scope

The user authorized the published Divar real-estate dataset as an initial source.
Its rows are real property **offers**, not requests by people seeking property.
Schema-v2 offer rows record `synthetic: false`, `derived: true`, and
`isNeedGroundTruth: false`; they must never be described as real user demand or
first-party need ground truth. Any first-person rewrite is synthetic. This
document covers only the real-estate auxiliary corpus for Si. No external
inference API was called for manual batch 02; its hypothetical Persian texts
were authored by the Codex assistant, not generated by Si, and were not
model-evaluated.

Si is a typed-decision model, not a text generator. It can return constrained
choices and yes/no/score decisions; it cannot faithfully rewrite each ad as a
natural Persian request. The converter keeps the real seller/agent state and
labels its output `divar-property-offer-facts/v2`, with
`perspective=seller_or_agent_supply_offer` and `isNeedGroundTruth=false`. It does
not create a buyer/tenant request or discover the seller's unknown buyer/renter
preferences.

### City and neighborhood coverage

Using exact catalog filenames, the existing Divar city-slug map, Divar's
slug-to-numeric-ID map, and the repo's explicit manual city map, 382 of the 421
source city slugs map to an existing app city catalog. Numeric Divar ID
evidence takes precedence over ambiguous manual transliterations; conflicts in
official slug/ID evidence remain unresolved. The remaining 39 stay unresolved;
they must not be fuzzy-forced into a different city. Those mapped slugs account
for 925,129 of the 999,416 output rows; 74,287 rows have no verified app city.
The source covers 421 cities out of 1,207 catalog cities, so it cannot provide
genuine source coverage for every app city.

437,139 rows have `neighborhood_slug`, but those values cover only 11 of the
421 source cities. The previous audit incorrectly reported zero app-catalog
matches because it checked only `{routeSlug}.json` and missed valid catalog
files such as `tehran-city.json`. A new public-metadata-only crosswalk joins
source slugs to Divar's current official district API, then compares the
official Persian district name to the correct app city catalog using exact
normalized names; it does not read listing titles/descriptions or use fuzzy
matching. It resolves 1,158 of 1,188 source city/neighborhood pairs to an
official district and maps 1,120 pairs (421,106 source rows) uniquely to an
app neighborhood. The remaining 68 pairs are unresolved by design: 30 source
slugs are absent from the current official district lists, 32 official names
have no exact app catalog name, and 6 would map ambiguously. See
[`data/divar/official-neighborhood-app-crosswalk-2026-09-25.json`](../data/divar/official-neighborhood-app-crosswalk-2026-09-25.json)
and its reproducible builder `scripts/datasets/build-divar-neighborhood-crosswalk.py`.

This crosswalk is a location reference only: the source still contains offers,
not genuine seeker requests, and the crosswalk is not integrated into the
training output or production resolver. Of 596,586 coordinate rows whose city
maps to the app catalog, only 154,660 land in exactly one existing neighborhood
bounding box; 259,527 intersect multiple boxes and 182,399 intersect none.
Bounding boxes are not authoritative, so they are not used to force a label.

## Si compatibility and measured baseline

The project venv contains `si==0.3.20`. Verified installed APIs:

- `si.load(model_id_or_path, device=...)`
- `Agent.predict(...)` and `Agent.predict_batch(...)`
- `si.common.build_sequence`, `build_model`, `proper_reward`, and `QTYPES`

The training primitives are in `si.common`, not top-level `si`; the runtime
package does not ship a trainer. The official notebook's decision-head recipe
must be adapted to the exact multilingual checkpoint and its own config/encoder
layout, then checked with a strict state-dict load, `si.load()` round-trip,
and held-out predictions. The installed `torch` reports MPS support was built
but `torch.backends.mps.is_available()` is false in this sandbox; CUDA is also
unavailable. This is an environment-specific probe and does not establish the
device availability of the already-running worker process.

The official Si fine-tuning notebook is not directly runnable for this goal:
it initializes `convaiinnovations/si` (the English checkpoint), trains on the
unrelated `LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions` benchmark, and launches on Kaggle with two
T4 GPUs. Its setup downloads model/data in Kaggle and its final cells can push a
checkpoint to the Hub. The project's installed `si==0.3.20` does expose
training primitives (`build_model`, `build_sequence`, `proper_reward`), but not
a supported trainer entry point. A project-specific training runner must be
validated against the exact cached `convaiinnovations/si-multilingual`
checkpoint and must stay local unless separate explicit transfer approval is
given. No model weights were loaded for this compatibility check and no
training was started.

Two local, PII-redacted probes used the cached
`convaiinnovations/si-multilingual` checkpoint, one 16-choice category
question, and stratified labels from the source metadata:

| Probe | Sample | Exact leaf-category accuracy | Mean inference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |

holdout cases.

## Safe conversion/training contract

1. Keep source text and every artifact local unless the user explicitly
   authorizes transfer. The cloud-GPU permission question is still pending.
2. Redact phone/email/URLs, normalize Persian text and digits, deduplicate and
   split by normalized text group before expanding one state into Si's typed
   decisions. Never allow duplicate text or conflicting labels across splits.
3. Keep Divar-derived offer examples in a separate auxiliary corpus with source
   row hash, source dataset/revision/hash, mapping version, and field-level
   provenance. Verbatim source-text rows are `synthetic=false`,
   `isNeedGroundTruth=false`, and `humanReviewed=false`; generated rewrites must
   be marked synthetic and never counted as actual demand.
4. Supervise only fields whose source semantics match the target decision.
   Source category may map to a hypothetical buy/rent category; area/rooms are
   offer facts. Budget preferences, amenity preferences, and user intent beyond
   category are not established by an offer and stay unknown.
5. City is a high-cardinality catalog lookup, not one Si choice among hundreds
   of values. Keep the user's selected city authoritative. Resolve neighborhoods
   by exact city-scoped aliases/catalog lookup; do not use uncertain coordinates
   or weak fuzzy matches as truth.
6. Keep an untouched test partition grouped by normalized source text; calibrate
   only on a separate calibration partition. Do not use an Si prediction as
   its own training label.
7. No training or model-quality claim is complete until per-field precision,
   recall, unknown/abstention behavior, macro accuracy, calibration, and
   need-style holdout results are measured.

The source card marks the database ODbL. Keep this derivative private during the
experiment; obtain qualified review of attribution/share-alike obligations and
individual text/privacy rights before redistributing source-derived data or a
trained artifact. See the [Divar dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/divarofficial/real_estate_ads),
[ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), and the
[official Si README](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/si).

The original Divar rows and their source labels are real offer data, but are not user needs. The old schema-v1 file is not a one-million-example need corpus. ### Si neighborhood-stratified shadow run (2026-09-26)

The exact local checkpoint `convaiinnovations/si-multilingual` was run on a
deterministic, explicitly hypothetical conversion of 1,496 deduplicated Divar
offers. Selection included category/city coverage plus one row per exact
crosswalk city/neighborhood pair. The artifact is
`data/divar/divar-hypothetical-needs-si-neighborhood-diverse-v1.jsonl`; its
manifest records the pinned input checksum, local MPS device, 597 batch calls,
1,104 ms mean reported batch latency, 10,421,476 output bytes, and
`trainingEligible: false`. It spans 16 property leaves and 382 app-city slugs;
1,120 unique exact city/neighborhood pairs in the 11 crosswalk-covered city
scopes are represented. This is a neighborhood-stratified diagnostic, not a
million-row corpus.

The reproducible aggregate evaluator is
`bun scripts/datasets/evaluate-divar-hypothetical.ts <proposal.jsonl>`. Si's
exact-choice agreement with the source-derived counterfactual labels was 80.82%
for category, 62.63% for property kind, and 34.49% for transaction type. These
are not independent or real-user accuracy scores: labels and first-person
templates are counterfactuals derived from seller-side offers. More importantly,
Si failed to abstain on omitted fields: false proposals among expected-unknown
rows were 98.99% for deed type, 99.36% for parking, 99.42% for elevator, and
99.56% for storage. The model therefore must not auto-apply these fields, and a
million-row Si pass would add cost and volume without trustworthy labels.

The exact-city-scoped deterministic neighborhood resolver was checked against
the 1,120 crosswalk-inserted catalog names: after regression fixes it resolved
1,120/1,120 to the expected canonical catalog id. This is a template/crosswalk
self-consistency result only, not evidence of 100% accuracy on natural user
text. The audit exposed two resolver defects and added tests: (1) a city name
also present as a broad catalog entry (e.g. Ahvaz) could become a competing
neighborhood; (2) overlapping district names with a city suffix could outrank
the intended exact district. The resolver now treats the selected city strictly
as scope, preserves parenthetical qualifiers in official names, and prefers an
overlapping city-independent canonical district over a city-suffixed duplicate.

The separate city-name self-consistency rate was 1,249/1,495 (83.55%) even
though a city label was present in the generated text. The remaining misses
were mostly small cities absent from the parser's current city-name index. In
the product flow a city already selected by the user remains authoritative and
must not be overwritten; broader text-only city recognition remains a distinct
coverage gap. The app-city-name bridge now also resolves exact catalog IDs such
as `tehran-city` to their canonical route slug without fuzzy guessing.

All 1,496 rows remain `synthetic: true`, `realNeedGroundTruth: false`, and
training-ineligible. The Divar snapshot is supply-side data; its ODbL
downstream-use, privacy, and individual content-rights review remain pending.
No first-party user data was used, no remote inference was called, and no
fine-tuning was performed. This output cannot be presented or counted as real
user demand or as approved training data.
